# HR Attrition Report

**Data:** IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition | **Employees:** 1,470 | **Leavers:** 237 | **Overall attrition rate: 16.1%**

## Executive summary
Attrition is not evenly spread. It concentrates in **Sales Representatives, Laboratory Technicians and HR roles**, among **new joiners (first year)**, **overtime workers**, and **entry-level, lower-paid staff**. Targeting these groups will cut turnover far more efficiently than company-wide measures.

All figures below are attrition **rates** (leavers / headcount in the segment).

## 5 key insights

**1. Sales Representatives are the highest-risk role (39.8%).**
That is about 2.5x the company average. The Sales department overall (20.6%) is the highest, but the problem is concentrated in this one role rather than all of Sales (Sales Executives are 17.5%).
*Action:* review targets, commission structure and workload for this role; run stay interviews.

**2. The first year is the danger zone (34.9%).**
Employees with 0-1 years of tenure leave at more than twice the company rate, and the rate falls steadily with tenure to 8.1% at 11+ years. 75 of the 237 leavers (32%) left within a year or less.
*Action:* strengthen onboarding, assign mentors, and hold 30/60/90-day check-ins.

**3. Overtime triples the likelihood of leaving (30.5% vs 10.4%).**
416 employees work overtime and 127 of them left, more than half of all leavers (54%).
*Action:* monitor overtime hours, rebalance workloads and review staffing in overtime-heavy teams.

**4. Entry-level and low-paid employees leave most.**
Job Level 1 has a 26.3% rate versus 9.7% at Level 2 and under 8% at Levels 4-5. The lowest income quartile has a 29.3% rate versus about 10% in the top two quartiles. Employees with no stock options leave at 24.4%, versus 7.6-9.4% for those with option levels 1-2.
*Action:* review pay competitiveness for entry roles and extend retention incentives (e.g. equity or bonuses) to junior staff.

**5. Travel and life circumstances amplify risk.**
Frequent travellers leave at 24.9% (vs 8.0% for non-travel). Single employees leave at 25.5% (vs 10.1% divorced and 12.5% married). Low job satisfaction (score 1) shows 22.8% versus 11.3% at score 4.
*Action:* limit unnecessary travel, add flexibility, and use satisfaction surveys as an early-warning signal. Marital status is context only and must not drive individual decisions.

## Recommendations
1. Prioritise a retention plan for **Sales Representatives** and **Laboratory Technicians**.
2. Redesign **first-year onboarding and early check-ins**.
3. Set an **overtime policy** with monitoring.
4. Review **entry-level compensation and incentives**.
5. Track attrition **rate** monthly by role, tenure and overtime on the dashboard.

## Method and limitations
- Rate = leavers / headcount in segment; company average 16.1% used as the benchmark.
- Small segments (e.g. Research Director n=80, HR n=52-63) have wider uncertainty; interpret with care.
- Findings show association, not causation. For example, overtime may reflect workload in already struggling teams.
- The dataset is synthetic (IBM), so results demonstrate analytical approach.
- Privacy: ID and constant columns excluded; only aggregated views are reported.
